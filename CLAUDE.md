## Concept

The CV's own line is the thesis: *"Most systems remember. I build systems that understand."*
Farhad's work (ontologies, graphs, governance layers) is not photographable, so instead of photos
each project's full-bleed plate is a **live generative Canvas 2D scene drawn in that project's own language**.
Never use stock imagery or abstract decoration. Every visual must be true to the work.

## Reference: camillemormal.com (inspected, not guessed)

- `document.body.scrollHeight === 0`. **No page scroll.** Fixed viewport, WebGL canvas full-bleed.
- Wheel/drag switches projects. Transition = plate **shrinks to an inset card → swaps → grows back** to full-bleed.
- Title centered, slides horizontally in the travel direction. `+` markers left/right = prev/next.
- Pagination `#h-pgn`: a digit column that rolls vertically, a `—` that scaleX-animates, and the total.
- Thumbnail filmstrip at the bottom. Percentage preloader.

## Hero (chessboard)

Design: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-23-chessboard-hero-design.md`. Mood reference is someone else's still, kept local and git-excluded; never commit it.

- Pieces: Cyrus = the Cyrus Cylinder, worn and broken at one end, in the centre (focus); Zagros = hex vault with five keys (three turned); OnePharma = graph lattice; Black Pearl = a dark milled block holding an open ivory shell with a black pearl; Falah = balance scale, tilted, coins on the low pan; Network Intrusion Detection = a round shield that has taken three arrows. The ivory is aged on purpose (grime, pitting, cracks). Farhad wants each piece to carry a story, not look clean.
- The board is blocks of stone at uneven heights (`build_board` in `blender/hero/scene.py`); each piece stands on the centre of one raised tile, or astride two (`PLACEMENT`, `PLATFORM`, `REACH`; Farhad: one tile rises on hover, two at most, never four). Tile heights step up to 0.44 so the levels throw shadows. The look is near-black with a hint of deep blue (Nov 24: Farhad asked for a dark blue feel; earlier he found a strong blue too much, so keep it a hint). In `hero.js` that is the fog colours (`LIGHT_COL`, `FOG_AMB`, `HAZE`) and a last grade (`heroLive.fxaa.uTint`, `uShadow`) that leaves the lit ivory alone. The fog has a shoulder (`heroLive.fog.uKnee`) so a bank crossing the light never burns to a white patch that travels: Farhad found that very bad. Block sides are drawn matte in `BOARD_FS`, not from the bake (their texture cells bled and read as glass), and the tops lose most of the baked grey mirror sheen (`heroLive.board.uMatte`); `uMatte` is 0 since the Nov 24 rebakes (the glass he meant was the mount, not the tiles). The fog is thick and frays at the edges (a third, finer noise layer, `FOG_FS` "wisps"); Farhad asked for it stronger and slowly passing. It lies mostly behind the pieces so the foreground keeps its blacks and the tile designs show. The march is equiangular (steps crowd where the ray passes the light) and its jitter is held still in time (`uJitT` 0): moving jitter read as rain in the bright fog under the cylinder. The bakes keep Blender's fog in (`bake_and_export`), because its scattered light is what lights the marble fronts; the tile inlay has a faint emission so designs read off the lit tiles. Polished marble (plinths, mount, the pearl's block: roughness about 0.2 with a clear coat, live `uSheen` 0.16) takes the light differently from satin ivory (0.06) and matte clay (0.03).
- One light only, hidden right behind the Cyrus Cylinder at the barrel's height (Farhad's call, after a reference still): the cylinder stands dark with a lit outline, rays fan through the fog, the sides fall to blue-black. No spots, no fill.
- Fog is low, patchy banks: a noise-driven volume in Blender, and in `hero.js` a live volume marched every frame (`FOG_FS`, at 1/`FOG_DIV` = a third of the size) lit by the same point light (`LIGHT`). The shafts come from the march itself: every step tests the way to the light against the six pieces (`lightVis`: a soft upright footprint of each piece's bounding box, up to its top), so a piece darkens only the fog between it and the camera on the far side from the light, never the fog beside, behind or above it. The old screen-space shaft pass is gone: it counted the near board as a blocker and blacked out all foreground fog. Fog in a piece's shadow is near black, as in Blender (ambient `FOG_AMB` is tiny; `uShadowFloor` 0.02 of the light gets through). The banks drift and churn slowly (broad and fine noise move in different directions, the fine one warped by the broad one); `heroLive.fog.uDrift` scales the speed. Don't go back to a flat pre-rendered fog image: Farhad found it read as a picture with pieces popping out. Bloom as in the posters (`output.py` `_glow`): above 0.38 bleeds out, a wide glow (sigma h/60, at an eighth of the size, weight 0.85) and a tight one (about h/300, a quarter of the size, 0.35), added in the last pass. Tune live through `window.heroLive.fog`, `.post`, `.fxaa` (also `uWideK`, `uTightK`) and `.bright` (`uThresh`). The backing buffer is capped by the sharpness level (pixel ratio and total pixels, see below), because every pass but the scene scales with pixels.
- The cylinder's mesh (Nov 24): 60 rings by 96 segments (13,220 triangles; it was 23 by 72). At 23 rings each ring was a corner on the outline against the light and the fine surface noise aliased into a zigzag, which is what Farhad kept seeing as jagged edges; sharper rendering only showed it more (he judged the lowest sharpness level the best). The posters were not re-rendered after this (`--stage export` only), so they still show the coarse cylinder.
- Sharpness levels (Nov 24; Farhad still saw zigzag outlines on his Retina screen after the coverage fix). The cause was resolution: one cap of 1.5x / 3.0 MP drew a large Retina window below 1x and stretched it. `LEVELS` in `hero.js` now lists [pixel ratio cap, buffer cap, frame budget ms], best first: 2x / 7.5 MP / 11.5, 2x / 5 MP / 11.5, 1.5x / 3 MP / 20. The mount test (median of seven timed frames after two warm-ups) takes the first level inside its budget and falls back to the poster only if the last fails. The fog never marches more than `FOG_MAX` (0.34 M) texels: its divisor grows with the buffer (`fogDiv`, recompiles `DIV`), and from `DIV` 4 the upsample is 3x3. Every level keeps 4x MSAA: a first try used 2x above 1.6x and Farhad then judged the low level the best of the three. On localhost a readout in the hero's bottom-left corner shows the level, the buffer size and frames a second, and keys 1 / 2 / 3 switch level (never shown on the live site). `heroLive.level` and `.mountMs` say what was picked, `heroLive.setLevel(i)` forces one. Touch screens (`pointer:coarse`, chosen once at mount) use `TOUCH_LEVELS` instead: 2x / 1.6 MP / 14, 1.6x / 1.1 MP / 16, 1.25x / 0.6 MP / 22. Phones used to get a flat 1.25x, which an iPhone stretched 2.4x: blurred and jaggy (Farhad, Nov 24). 2x is the top on purpose. Phone cost at 2x is not measured; the fog march costs about 2.6x what it did at 1.25x. A runtime step-down (`stepDown`, called from `frame()`) drops one level when the smoothed frame interval stays above 40 ms (or above 28 ms and erratic) for 3 s, at most once per 5 s, never to the poster; a steady 33 ms (iOS Low Power Mode) is left alone, and it is off while a level is forced by key. `resize()` returns early when size, pixel ratio and orientation are unchanged (iOS fires resize whenever the Safari toolbar moves). Depth of field (`uMaxR`) and the wide glow follow the focal length in backing pixels in portrait; landscape keeps the old height rule (13 px and h/60 at 1080 high). Phones default to subtle grain. `?why` on the URL prints the ladder, phone blend, pixel ratio, buffer, MSAA and half float. The frame costs in this file predate this and today's other additions (about +18% at the old level); they were not re-measured on an idle GPU, because Farhad's own tab was rendering while the tests ran.
- Smooth outlines against the glow (Nov 24, Farhad: the edge of Cyrus against the halo was zigzagged): the fog is added by a single depth sample, so on a silhouette it came in whole pixels and undid the MSAA. Every scene material now writes its view depth / `ZS` (60, the far plane) to alpha; the MSAA resolve averages it, and `POST_FS` blends the near and far surfaces' finished composites by that coverage wherever the depth jumps (blend the composites, not the fog values: those are stored as square roots, and blending them gave a half-covered pixel a quarter of the glow, which still read as steps). The scene target is half float when `EXT_color_buffer_float` exists, for alpha precision.
- Smooth outlines: the scene renders with 4x MSAA. Each fog texel marches to the depth of one exact full-size pixel, and the composite upsamples the fog depth-aware (4x4 texels weighted by distance and depth likeness, nearest-depth fallback), so fog never bleeds blocks across a silhouette. Depth of field: where a blurred background pixel touches a nearer, sharper piece, it blurs at full strength and the piece's share of the pixel (estimated from the two neighbours across the edge) is laid back on top, so the MSAA edge survives with no sharp ring. The fog upsample takes 3x3 texels and works out each texel's position from the pixel index, so it lines up at any buffer size. A last FXAA pass (`FXAA_FS`, then bloom and dither) smooths what the single-sample depth puts back. Without half-float targets the frame buffer is 8-bit, so `POST_FS` dithers too (`DITHER_POST`).
- Materials differ on purpose: Cyrus is fired clay with uneven lengthwise lines of writing (a shader bump, `clay_lines`) on an opaque veined marble mount. Mounts, plinths and the pearl's block are marble, not translucent or glossy: Farhad found the old alabaster mount read as crystal (Nov 24). The ivory keeps only a little translucency (`sss` 0.14).
- The poster (`poster.webp`, or `poster-portrait.webp` under `(orientation: portrait)`) is only the fallback. A script before `<header>` runs the same device test as `boot()` before first paint: where the live scene will be tried it sets `html.hero-wait`, which hides the poster, so the hero starts dark and the scene fades in (`.hero-stage` transition). `heroPosterBack()` shows the poster again on any failure (load, context loss, too slow, no layout), after 6 s from first paint if the mount has not started, and 8 s after the mount starts if it is still not up. That script sits first in the page, right after the import map and before the font stylesheet, and on those devices it preloads `hero.js`, three.js, GLTFLoader, the meshopt decoder and `pieces.glb`, so they are fetched ahead of the hidden poster. `boot()` starts on `layout.json`, not on window `load`. The poster `<img>` carries no `fetchpriority`; on devices that never try the scene the same script adds `<link rel=preload as=image fetchpriority=high>` for the poster that fits the orientation, so it is still their fast first paint, while on the others the hidden poster does not compete with three.js and `pieces.glb`. Devices that never try (reduced motion, weak, no WebGL2) see the poster from first paint. It always fits the width, so no piece is cropped.
- Three touches added Nov 24 (Farhad picked them from a list of ideas). **The entrance** (replaced "the light comes on" the same day: Farhad did not like the flicker): the light comes up once, smoothly, in about 1.4 s (`igniteAt` in `hero.js`, applied as `uIgnite` in `FXAA_FS`; dim parts go as its square so the halo comes first). Then five pieces rise into place from a little below (0.26 to 0.45, with their plinths and tiles), each with its own delay, distance and time (`ENTER`: two pairs start almost together, the shield lands last); Cyrus stands still. About a third of the free board blocks start 0.05 to 0.2 high or low and settle once (`TILE_IN`, attribute `aIn` on the board, picked by a fixed hash so it is the same every visit). The light is mostly up before anything moves, or the motion would be lost in the dark. All done by about 3.6 s; hover lift waits until a piece has landed. Replay with `heroLive.ignite()`; `heroLive.ignite(1.5)` holds it at 1.5 s. The poster has no entrance. **The cursor's wake**: moving the mouse over the hero lays lines of sight evenly along its path (`WAKE_STEP` apart, up to `WAKE_N` 16, a new one takes the faintest slot); each eases in, pushes the fog the way the cursor went and cuts it a little, then fades (`WAKE_LIFE` 1.4 s) and widens so the fog closes back in (`WAKE_GLSL`, used by `FOG_FS`; overlapping pushes are averaged, not summed). Farhad found the first version glitchy: lines dropped every 60 ms with a hard cut read as beads and pops. The halo round the light is barely cut: cutting it read as the light dimming. **Dust**: `MOTES` (420) flakes in a box round the light, lit only by it with the same piece-shadow test as the fog (`LIGHT_VIS`) and a steep falloff, so only the air near the light shows any; the wake pushes them. Farhad found the first version fake (blinking, crisp star-like dots spread everywhere): now they glint slowly, are never sharper than a soft dot, and drift on two octaves of slow wander. They are drawn into `frameRT` after the depth of field (which smeared them to nothing), test the scene depth themselves and grow into their own bokeh disc. Tune with `heroLive.motes.uGain` (1.2).
- **Moving parts on hover** (Nov 24, idea 5). Each piece is still one mesh and one bake; faces of a part are tagged in `pieces.py` (`_tag`, exported as COLOR_0, red = part / 8) and its motion is a JSON `rig` in the node extras (`_rig`, glTF local coordinates). `PIECE_VS` (define `RIG`) turns a part about its pivot or makes it hang from an anchor on another part; `hero.js` drives the angles with a spring per part (`k`, `c`) or a one-off damped shake (`quiver`). Cyrus rolls 9° in its saddles; the fifth Zagros key (facing the camera) turns 90°, four of five; the OnePharma graph turns 40° on its stem; the pearl's upper shell opens 11° wider; Falah's beam sinks 6° more and rocks to rest, its pans hanging level from the ends; the first arrow in the shield quivers. The baked light moves with a part, so keep the angles small (the key's turn is the exception, and it is a ring).
- Film grain (Nov 24, from Farhad's `grainy-gradients` skill): the skill's feTurbulence tile (0.8, 3 octaves, 200 CSS px, rasterised at backing resolution) laid over the frame last in `FXAA_FS`. Default mode is even: it moves L* by the same amount everywhere (Medium = 0.8 L* on sky, glow and lit floor alike); true black is left alone and average brightness does not move. The skill's own overlay pick gave the sky 0.8 but the lit floor 3.9, so it is kept only to compare. Levels `GRAIN` in `hero.js` (subtle / medium / strong / overlay / off), less on HDR screens and with high contrast. Switch live with `heroLive.grain("strong")` or `?grain=off` in the URL. The poster has no grain.
- Six `a[data-piece]` links in `nav.hero-pieces` are placed over the pieces from `layout.json` when only the poster shows. While the live scene runs they come from `heroLive.boxes()` instead (each piece and plinth box, risen by its lift, projected through the live camera; hero.js calls `opts.onBoxes` after a resize and at most every 0.12 s while the pan, scroll or a lift moves them); a link whose piece is out of frame gets `tabindex=-1`. They are the keyboard and screen-reader route and the tap targets when only the poster shows. Each goes to `#proj-<id>`.
- Phone framing (Nov 24, Farhad on his iPhone: pieces tiny and far, title too big). Narrow portrait blends from the poster's portrait camera to `PHONE_CAM` (0,1.45,8.6) looking at (0,0.35,-1.0), 32° across: fully at aspect `PHONE_AT[0]` 0.6 and below, none from 0.72, so portrait tablets still see all six. Cyrus is about 1.8x wider, the band sits at about 34-56% of the height, and Zagros and the shield start out of frame. A sideways drag (past 8 px, mostly sideways; `touch-action:pan-y` keeps upright drags for the page) turns the look in place about the camera's own position (`rotateOnWorldAxis`), never an orbit, so the bakes still hold; it glides after release and stops at `panLo`/`panHi`, where the outer piece's edge is `PAN_MARGIN` (8°, was 5° before the tour) inside the frame. The one-time nudge is gone: the tour replaced it. The phone CSS (`(orientation:portrait) and (max-width:600px)`) shrinks the title block (top 10svh, mark 44 px, title 22 px) and widens the vignette. Not yet checked on a device.
- Phone tour (Nov 24, Farhad: two pieces were reachable only by a swipe nobody finds). Wherever the phone framing leaves the row wider than the screen (`(orientation:portrait) and (max-aspect-ratio:18/25)`, i.e. under `PHONE_AT[1]` 0.72: phones, and an iPad mini at 0.66; `heroTourQ` in site.html) pin the hero 400svh (min 2240px): of the 3 extra screens, `HERO_TOUR_SHARE` (2.3/3) is the tour and the rest the exit, as on desktop (`heroSplit`). `feedScroll` sends `setScroll(exit, tour)`; desktop sends no second value and nothing changes. `TOUR` in hero.js: seven stops (Cyrus with the title, Zagros, Black Pearl, Cyrus, OnePharma, Falah, the shield), each held for the first `hold` (0.45) of its seventh then an eased turn; a stop's angle is the piece's direction from the live camera (`ang`), clamped to the pan limits (Zagros and the shield stop short of centre). The stop's piece is presented like a hover (lift, focus, label, moving parts) and its bake lifted by `TOUR.gain` (1.2-1.3, Cyrus 1) through the piece shader's `GAIN` define: `uGain` raises the dim side of the texture only (smoothstep 0.35-0.9 of the brightest channel), no light added. A swipe adds an offset (`panGoal`) on top of the tour angle (`panBase`); once the page scrolls 0.01 of either progress since the swipe ended it eases home in 0.6 s; while off the path by more than 1.5° the piece nearest the centre is presented. A focused link (`setHover`) turns the look to its piece the same way. On phones the ripple and the pieces' rise play in the first stop, the lean-in is half spread over the tour and half in the exit, the look turns back to centre over the first 0.6 of the exit and nothing is presented after 0.3. The title clears over the hold on the first stop. The label sits under the presented plinth with its number (`.hero-label.under`, kept on screen). `heroLive.tour()` prints the stop angles. The label is set again from the scene whenever a link takes or loses focus on the tour. Not yet checked on a device.
- Pinned scroll (Nov 24). Where the live scene is tried, `html.hero-pin` (set with `hero-wait`) makes `.hero` 170svh (phones 400svh, see the phone tour) and `.hero-pin` (everything in the hero, gradients included) sticks for the extra 0.7 of a screen. `.hero` must not get `overflow:hidden` again: that makes it the scroll box and sticky never sticks; `.hero-pin` clips. `feedScroll` in site.html sends 0..1 over the first 0.7 of the extra height to `heroLive.setScroll` (raw; hero.js eases after it with `SCROLL.ease` and takes the first value as is). The choreography, all in `SCROLL`: the camera leans in (`dolly` 0.14 of its distance, eased in-out) and the look dips 0.25; free blocks ripple out from the cylinder (`aScr`: delay and amount per block from the JS hash, 0.06-0.36, three in ten sink, none right in front of the lens: `uNearZ`); pieces rise 0.18 with their platforms as the ripple reaches them, Cyrus no more than its hover 0.06 (the light hides behind it); from 0.35 the banks thicken (`uDense`) and rise (`uRise`), from 0.55 the light goes down 55% (`lightK`: fog, block sides and dust; not `uIgnite`, which fades the whole frame). The title and cue clear over the first 0.3 of the extra height and the picture fades from 0.8 until half the pin has scrolled away. `heroPosterBack()` takes the pin off only while `scrollY` < half a screen (else the page would jump) and then starts the poster's push-in (`heroPosterPush`, scale 1.06, unpinned only); a mount that comes up after a timer gave up pins again under the same rule. Reduced motion and poster-first devices never pin. Amplitudes, fog at 0.6-0.9 and the fade timing are not yet checked by eye.
- **Loader** (Nov 24, Farhad: visitors kept getting the poster or a dark wait; spec `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-24-hero-loader-design.md`). Wherever the scene is tried and the URL has no `#`, the hero itself is the loader (`html.hero-load`, `hero-loading`). Only the name shows, in its own place, sliding up in CSS (`heroName`, not GSAP), with a hairline bar and "Loading" (`.hero-loader`) where the subtitle sits. On reveal the bar fades, the subtitle slides in, the mark grows (`mark.js` waits on `window.heroReady`) and the scene fades in with `heroLive.ignite()`. The bar is real: resource timing for the modules and three.js, `pieces.glb` bytes (`mountHero` `onProgress`), then the mount. It is shown for at least 1.8 s (`LOAD_MIN`). Scroll is held by blocking wheel, touch and keys, never by `overflow:hidden` on `<html>` (that empties every IntersectionObserver's root and nothing mounts). Don't give an element a class that is also a class on `<html>` (`.hero-load` once hid the whole page). The choice (`html.hero-ask`, **Load full experience** / **Optimised for this device**) shows for weak devices before anything downloads, when the speed test says `too slow`, and after 12 s with no progress (`LOAD_STALL`). Full mounts with `force` (`mountTest(w, 0, force)`: the lowest level that fits, never too slow); optimised shows the poster. The 6 s and 8 s give-up timers apply only without the loader.
- `hero.js` mounts only when: motion is allowed, WebGL2 exists, the device is not weak (under 4 GB or 4 cores; phones are tried since Nov 24 and the mount speed test sends a slow one to the poster), and the hero is within one viewport. It falls back to the poster on load failure, context loss, or if six timed frames at mount average over 20 ms. Frame cost (Nov 24, M1 Pro, Chrome, forced by a 1-pixel readPixels, all passes incl. bloom and FXAA): 5.6 ms at 2160x1350, 6.3 ms at the 3.0 MP cap (2309x1299, what a 1920x1080 or larger window at DPR 1.5 gets), 3.4 ms at 1536x1152.
